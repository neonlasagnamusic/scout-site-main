Pasta Bar

Harder to portion but easy to serve. Cook pasta ahead of time and chill in cooler. Dip in hot water to warm at camp.

Moderate

Sauce Options

  • Thai Peanut Sauce
  • Meat Sauce
  • Alfredo
  • Pesto

Serving Sizes

  • 2 oz dry spaghetti per person
  • ½ cup (4 oz) sauce per person

Camping Tips

  • Precook spaghetti, rinse well, toss with olive oil — reheat in boiling water for 45 seconds at camp
  • Use precooked frozen meatballs: boil in a food-safe bag for 20 minutes, or simmer in sauce for 20 minutes

Taco Bar

Easy to portion, cook meat ahead of time, easy to serve. Reheat meat with an immersion cooker and large tub. Use diced baked tofu as a beef alternative.

Easy

Crockpot Taco Meat Serves 40

Ingredients

  • 5 lbs lean ground beef or baked tofu
  • 2 tbsp garlic powder
  • 1 cup taco seasoning
  • 1 liter water
  • 40 corn or flour tortillas

Condiments

  • 2 heads of lettuce
  • 1 yellow onion
  • 1 bundle cilantro
  • 24 oz salsa
  • 1 bottle hot sauce

Instructions

  1. Place ground beef into slow cooker. Top with taco seasoning, garlic powder, and salsa.
  2. Cover and cook on low for 6 hours or high for 3 hours.
  3. When finished, drain any liquid and break up beef with a fork as desired.
  4. Serve with corn or flour tortillas and condiments.

Gear

You don't need expensive gear for a great campout. Here's a practical beginner checklist.

Sleeping

  • Tent (2–3 person for a family of 3–4)
  • Sleeping bag rated to 30–40°F
  • Sleeping pad or air mattress
  • Pillow (camp or from home)

Clothing (Per Person)

  • Layers — mornings and evenings get cold
  • Rain jacket or poncho
  • Sturdy closed-toe shoes or hiking boots
  • Hat for sun protection

Cooking

  • Camp stove + fuel canister (or shared)
  • Pots, pans, utensils
  • Plates, bowls, cups (bring reusable or use paper)
  • Firestarter, lighter or matches

Essentials

  • First aid kit
  • Sunscreen and bug spray
  • Headlamp or flashlight
  • Water bottles (at least 1 per person)

Camping Gear for First-Time Families

Camping your first time can be scary to prepare for, but with a few tips, trial and error on what works for you and your family, it can be pretty easy.

These recommendations are for the Scouting family that believes they will be an active participant and eventually transition from Cub Scouts to Boy Scouts. The goals are to minimize cost and minimize redundant gear across multiple use cases. Better gear that lasts longer is recommended where it counts.

Unfortunately, there probably isn't a one-size-fits-all tent. A typical active scouting family may end up with a family camping tent, a 2 lb backpacking tent, and a 2-person tent for car camping with Boy Scouts. If you're on a budget, splurge on the lightweight backpacking tent (~$200) and go budget on the others.

Cub Scout Family Camping 2 adults + 2 kids

Get a tent you can stand up in with room for gear — size up 1–2 sizes from your headcount. For 4 people, a 5 or 6-person tent is ideal. Expect to pay $150+ at Big 5 or Walmart. With a bigger tent, you'll also want:

  • Doormat — to leave shoes on and wipe off dust
  • Brush and dustpan
  • Cots or queen-size air mattress
  • Tent footprint

Bear through AOL & Boy Scout Camping 1 adult + 1 kid

Our Boy Scout troop provides tents for Scouts but not adults — and Boy Scouts and adults should not share a tent. Our Cub Scout Pack does not provide tents; Cub Scouts sleep with their parents. A 4-person tent fits both roles well — it can just barely fit a queen-size mattress and is small enough to carry several hundred yards. Expect to pay $50+.

BSA has two types of campouts: some where moving gear from transportation to campsite is under half a mile (car camping), and some where you carry everything on your back (backpacking). New Scouts will spend their first few years mostly car camping. For beginners, get gear they can make mistakes with — when they start backpacking, they'll upgrade to higher-end gear.

Mandatory starter gear:

Sleeping Bag 20° rating, 4 lbs, ~1 cubic foot or smaller  under $90
Sleeping Pad Must have an R-value (thermal resistance) rating. Folding foam type is 10× faster to set up and pack than inflatable  under $60
Water Bottle Wide-mouth Nalgene — lasts forever, compatible with water filters  under $15
Duffel Bag With removable shoulder strap — big enough to hold 3–4 sleeping bags  under $40
Hiking Boots Ankle height, water-resistant preferred. Kids' feet grow fast — buy expecting 6 months of use
